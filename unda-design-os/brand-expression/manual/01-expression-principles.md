# 1. Expression Principles

These govern every brand asset — a photo, an illustration, a slide, a business card. Where [Creative Principles](../../creative-direction/creative-principles.md) governs interface design, these seven govern *expression*: the outward-facing material a person encounters before or alongside the product.

## Clarity Over Decoration

**Explanation** — every asset should communicate its one idea before it impresses. If a viewer has to decode the visual before understanding the message, the visual has failed.

**Rationale** — Unda operates across four divisions with different audiences (enterprise buyers, developers, travelers). The one thing they all need is to understand quickly what they're looking at. Decoration is a tax on that.

**Examples** — a case study slide that leads with the outcome number, large, before any supporting chart. A social graphic with one headline and one supporting image, not five competing elements.

**When to use** — always; this is the default lens for every asset.

**When not to use** — never fully suspended, but Unda Studio's more editorial register (see [Cross-brand consistency](../../creative-direction/cross-brand-consistency.md)) can let a striking image carry more of the communication load than text — clarity through image instead of through text is still clarity.

## Systems Over Noise

**Explanation** — every asset is built from the same tokens, grid, and component vocabulary as every other asset. Nothing is a one-off creative flourish invented for a single deliverable.

**Rationale** — four divisions, dozens of asset types, produced by different people over years, must still look like one company. That only survives if everyone draws from the same system rather than each person interpreting the brand freshly each time.

**Examples** — a conference banner uses the same type scale and color tokens as the website hero it's advertising. A pitch deck's chart colors are the same `status.*`/division-accent tokens used in the product's own dashboards.

**When to use** — always, for any asset that will be seen more than once or by more than one person.

**When not to use** — genuine one-off internal experiments (a design exploration never meant to ship) don't need to honor the system — but label them clearly as exploratory so they're never mistaken for approved direction.

## Motion With Purpose

**Explanation** — see [Motion foundations](../../design-system/foundations/motion.md) and [Motion direction](../../creative-direction/motion-direction.md); this principle extends the same idea to marketing and brand assets (video, animated social content, presentation transitions), not just interface motion.

**Rationale** — Unda's calm, unhurried emotional register (see [Creative vision](../../creative-direction/creative-vision.md)) is easy to break with motion that exists to look impressive rather than to communicate.

**Examples** — a logo animation that resolves the void into the "U" shape once, to signal "loading complete" — not a looping idle animation that exists purely for visual interest. A slide transition that's a simple cut or fade, not a 3D flip.

**When to use** — any moving asset: video intros/outros, animated social posts, presentation builds, product demo recordings.

**When not to use** — never use motion to disguise a weak idea; if the static version of the asset doesn't work, animating it won't fix that.

## Precision Through Restraint

**Explanation** — fewer, better-chosen elements beat more elements. A single strong photograph beats a collage; one clear chart beats three overlapping ones.

**Rationale** — matches [Design constraints](../../creative-direction/design-constraints.md): "Unda is... confident enough to leave space empty." Restraint reads as competence, not as having less to say.

**Examples** — a pitch deck slide with one metric at `type.display` size and nothing else fighting for attention. A business card with only the required information, generous margins, nothing added to "fill the space."

**When to use** — always, especially under pressure to "add more" to a deliverable.

**When not to use** — reference-heavy documentation (API tables, changelogs) legitimately needs density — restraint there means no *unnecessary* elements, not no elements.

## Confidence Without Arrogance

**Explanation** — state facts plainly. Let the work speak. Never oversell, never use superlatives the evidence doesn't support, never talk down to the audience.

**Rationale** — matches the brand promise (["thoughtful solutions... to help businesses move forward with confidence"](../../brand/overview.md)) and the desired emotional response of meeting "a highly competent, unhurried expert" ([Creative vision](../../creative-direction/creative-vision.md)).

**Examples** — a case study titled with the actual measured outcome ("Cut deployment time by 40%"), not a vague superlative ("Revolutionary results!"). A testimonial page using named, attributable quotes only (see [Testimonials pattern](../../design-system/patterns/testimonials.md)) — a vague quote undermines more trust than it builds.

**When to use** — always, in every piece of outward-facing copy and imagery.

**When not to use** — internal, informal communication can loosen this; it's a rule for brand-facing expression, not for how the team talks to each other.

## Human Optimism

**Explanation** — Unda's work is ultimately about helping real people and organizations move forward. Expression should feel hopeful and constructive, never cynical, alarmist, or cold.

**Rationale** — the [mission and vision](../../brand/overview.md) center on empowerment and long-term trust, not disruption-for-its-own-sake or fear-based selling ("without X you'll fail").

**Examples** — a Technologies-division piece framing automation as freeing people for higher-value work, not replacing them. A Travel-division piece framing travel as connection and discovery, not just logistics.

**When to use** — copywriting tone, photography subject choice, illustration mood.

**When not to use** — case studies and error states should still be honest about problems and limitations — optimism about outcomes doesn't mean glossing over real constraints.

## African Innovation, Global Execution

**Explanation** — Unda's [vision](../../brand/overview.md) is explicit: "to become one of Africa's most trusted technology companies, recognized globally." Expression should reflect that origin with pride and specificity, never as a footnote and never as a stereotype.

**Rationale** — this is a genuine differentiator, not decoration: it tells a real story about where craftsmanship this rigorous comes from, and it matters to the audiences (African businesses, the diaspora, global partners) the brand actually serves.

**Examples** — [photography](./03-photography-direction.md) that shows real African workplaces, cities, and professionals at the same quality bar as any global tech brand's imagery — competence and modernity, not the "poverty and landscape" clichés international media defaults to. Case studies naming real markets and real outcomes rather than a generic "global" framing that erases where the work actually happens.

**When to use** — photography subject selection, case study framing, company-story copy, conference/event presence.

**When not to use** — never use origin as a lowered bar ("impressive for an African company") — the standard is the global standard, full stop; origin is a fact about identity, not a qualifier on quality.
