# 7. Graphic Language

Reusable graphic assets — everything here derives from the logo's own geometry ([Logo system](../../brand/logo-system.md): the 8×8 construction grid, the 14×14-unit module with 3-unit corner radius, and the Radial Frame concept of four modules around a shared void) rather than introducing an unrelated decorative vocabulary.

## Why derive from the logo

A graphic language invented independently of the mark risks feeling like a second, competing identity. Deriving every pattern from the logo's own grid and module shape means any graphic element — a background, a divider, a frame — reads as unmistakably Unda even without the logo present.

## Modular patterns & grids

A repeating field of the logo's module shape (rounded square, same corner radius as the mark) at reduced opacity, used as a subtle background texture on dark surfaces (decks, dark-mode marketing sections) — never at a scale or contrast that competes with foreground content.

## Backgrounds

Flat, quiet, single-color or a very subtle neutral gradient-free tone shift. No applied noise, no gradients-as-backgrounds ([Always avoid: layout](../../creative-direction/design-constraints.md)). The module-pattern above is the *only* approved textured background.

## Dividers

A single hairline (`border.hairline`, 1px) is the default divider — matches every component's existing divider treatment. Where a heavier visual break is needed (between major deck sections, for instance), a short accent-colored rule derived from the arc's stroke weight (3 units, scaled) may be used — never a full-width heavy rule.

## Radial systems

The logo's own radial arrangement (four corner elements around a center) can generalize into a compositional device for grids of four related items (the four divisions, a four-step process) — arranged in the same corner-orientation the symbol uses, not a literal enlarged logo.

## Overlays

Where text must sit over a photograph, use a flat neutral scrim at `opacity.scrim` (0.4) — same token the [Modal component](../../design-system/components/modal-drawer-popover-and-tooltip.md) uses for its backdrop. No gradient overlays, no colored duotone treatments.

## Brand frames

A frame built from four corner modules (echoing the logo's four modules, at `radius.lg` corner treatment) may border a hero image or a featured case study — never a decorative frame unrelated to the module shape.

## Abstract shapes

Limited to the rounded-square and arc primitives already defined by the logo and the interface radius scale. No arbitrary blob shapes, no unrelated geometric motifs — if a shape doesn't trace back to an existing primitive, it doesn't belong in this system.
