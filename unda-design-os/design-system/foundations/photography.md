# Photography

## Philosophy

Show reality with intention. Photography builds trust in a way illustration can't — it documents genuine moments, real work, real people. Avoid overly staged or exaggerated imagery; people should believe what they see.

## Style: editorial minimalism

Natural lighting, real environments, genuine interactions, clean composition, limited distractions, neutral color grading, strong use of negative space. The reference point is high-quality editorial photography, not commercial stock imagery.

## Composition

Simplicity (one clear subject), balance (thoughtful framing), whitespace (room for typography where relevant), natural perspective (avoid overly dramatic angles), subtle depth of field, and every image should communicate a clear idea or emotion — not just look nice.

## People

Authentic expressions, diverse representation across background/profession/age/ability/culture — respectful, not performative. Preferred subjects: teams collaborating, people actually doing the work (designing, engineering, traveling), product demonstrations.

## Product photography

Use real product imagery wherever possible — actual software interfaces, actual devices in use — rather than placeholder mockups.

## Color treatment

Neutral white balance, natural skin tones, soft contrast, moderate saturation, consistent exposure. Avoid heavy filters, excessive HDR, extreme grading, artificial lighting effects — anything that reads as a trend from a specific year.

## Photography by division

- **Unda Studio** — creativity in action (design reviews, sketching, workshops).
- **Unda Technologies** — engineering and product development (developers working, code reviews, demos).
- **Unda Travel** — real travel experiences (airports, hotels, business travelers, destinations) — explicitly avoid generic vacation stock photography.
